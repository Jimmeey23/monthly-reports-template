01 · Executive Summary

July delivered the highest attendance in eight months but the lowest revenue of 2026 — the studio has a conversion problem, not a demand problem.

Headline revenue contracted to ₹14.13L, down -32.0% from June and -33.4% below the Jan – Mar baseline. The proximate cause is buyer count: only 128 unique buyers transacted — the lowest of 2026 — even as visits hit 2,432 at 47.3% fill. The conversion rate halved to 9.9%. The structural positive is discount discipline: at ₹1.51L and ₹9.35 efficiency, July is the most discount-disciplined month of the year.

Key Insights · July 2026
01
Attendance is at an eight-month high — revenue is not.
July delivered 2,432 visits across 368 sessions at 47.3% fill — the highest volume and utilisation since January. Yet net sales fell to ₹14.13L, down -32.0% MoM. The studio is monetising attendance less efficiently than at any point in 2026.

04
Buyer count contracted sharply.
Only 128 unique buyers transacted in July, down from 217 in June (-41.0% MoM) and -31.7% below the Jan–Mar baseline. The transaction count fell to 200. This is the proximate cause of the revenue decline — fewer people are buying, even as more people are attending.
05
Churn rate is stable but the lapsed book is growing.
298 members lapsed in July at a 40.8% churn rate — broadly stable vs June’s 42.7%. But cumulative unique lapsed members now stand at 978, and only 208 active memberships remain. The renewal engine renewed 411 of 730 expirations (56.3%), but the gap between lapses and renewals is widening in absolute terms.
06
June was a strong month that set a high bar.
June delivered ₹20.77L net sales with 31 conversions at 24.2%, 2,281 visits, and 217 buyers — all above baseline. July’s decline is measured against a genuinely strong June, not a weak one. The two-month picture is one of a strong June followed by a volume-led but revenue-soft July.

What worked / What did not work

A balanced read on July 2026 — every item below is anchored to a number in the headline KPI table above.

+
Visit volume and fill rate hit eight-month highs.
2,432 visits across 368 sessions at 47.3% fill — up from 2,281 visits at 46.3% in June. Only 16 empty sessions. The schedule is delivering attendance at a rate the studio has not seen since January.
+
Discount discipline fully restored post-anniversary.
Discount value at ₹1.51L is -34% below June and -60% below the Jan–Mar baseline. Discount efficiency at ₹9.35 is the best reading of 2026 — every rupee of discount now generates 9.4x in net revenue.
+
Lead pipeline grew month-on-month.
151 leads in July vs 128 in June — a 18% increase. The top-of-funnel is not the constraint; the breakdown is in the conversion stage, which is a more addressable problem than lead scarcity.
+
Average transaction value held above baseline.
ATV at ₹7,433 is 5.1% above the Jan–Mar baseline of ₹7,076, despite the buyer contraction. Those who are buying are spending at healthy ticket sizes — the issue is buyer count, not pricing.
+
Renewal volume kept pace with rising expirations.
411 renewals from 730 expirations (56.3%) — renewal rate held within 0.4pp of June despite a 15% increase in expiration volume. The renewal engine is scaling with the membership base.
+
Late-cancel volume fell even as bookings rose.
295 late cancels vs 257 in June, despite 7% more bookings. Heavy cancelers (6+ cancels) fell from 6 to 4. Booking discipline is improving.
−
Net sales contracted 32% month-on-month.
₹14.13L vs ₹20.77L in June — a -32.0% decline. This is -33.4% below the Jan–Mar baseline, making July the weakest revenue month of 2026 outside of the seasonal lows.
−
Conversion rate halved to 9.9%.
15 conversions from 151 leads (9.9%) vs 31 from 128 (24.2%) in June. The lead-to-member conversion mechanism deteriorated sharply — the single biggest operational issue this month.
−
Buyer count fell to 128 — the lowest of 2026.
128 unique buyers is -41.0% below June and -31.7% below baseline. 200 transactions is also the lowest of the year. Attendance is up but the paying base is shrinking.
−
Website and walk-in channels converted zero leads.
25 Website leads and 7 walk-in leads each produced 0 conversions. Together, these channels consumed 32 leads (21% of the pipeline) and generated zero revenue — the largest leakage point in the funnel.
−
Hosted-class funnel continued to under-convert.
34 Hosted Class leads → only 1 conversion (2.9%). This is structurally consistent with May’s 3.4% rate. The hosted-class format generates attendance but not buyers — a persistent conversion gap.
−
Absolute lapsed count rose despite stable churn.
298 lapsed members vs 271 in June — a 10.0% increase. While the churn rate is stable at ~41%, the growing membership base means more absolute lapses each month. Cumulative unique lapsed now stands at 978.
−
Session revenue grew but sales revenue fell.
Session-attributed revenue rose to ₹21.03L (17.8% MoM), yet product sales fell to ₹14.87L. The gap suggests members are attending on existing packages rather than purchasing new ones — a consumption-over-acquisition pattern.

Data-backed conclusions for senior management

01 July is a volume-without-revenue month — the opposite of the May anniversary problem. The studio has never been busier (2,432 visits, 47.3% fill), yet net sales at ₹14.13L are the lowest of 2026. The issue is buyer count (128), not attendance or pricing.
02 The conversion funnel is the binding constraint. A 9.9% conversion rate with 151 leads means 136 leads entered the studio and left without buying. Restoring conversion to June’s 24.2% alone would add ~21 conversions and approximately ₹2.72L in monthly revenue.
03 Discount discipline is the one structural win to protect. July’s ₹9.35 discount efficiency is the best of 2026 and validates the post-May correction. Any short-term temptation to discount-drive revenue in a soft month would reverse this gain — the data is unambiguous.
04 June was a genuinely strong month — July’s decline is not a reversion to a weak norm. June’s ₹20.77L net, 31 conversions, and 217 buyers all exceeded baseline. The two-month picture is strong-then-soft, not a sustained deterioration — but the trend direction demands intervention.
05 The lapsed book is the largest unrealised revenue pool. 978 cumulative unique lapsed members — led by 221 1-Month Unlimited holders — represent a reactivation opportunity worth an estimated ₹35–50L in LTV recovery if even 15% are reactivated.
06 Attendance and sales are now decoupled — this is a strategic warning sign. When visits rise 7% MoM while sales fall 32%, the business model is leaking value at the attendance-to-purchase boundary. The fix is not more attendance — it is better conversion of existing attendance into recurring revenue.
07 The studio is one conversion-focused quarter from a structural step-up. If the conversion rate is restored to 20%+ and the discount discipline is held, the attendance base of 2,400+ monthly visits provides the raw material for ₹25–30L net sales — a 75–110% lift from July’s ₹14.13L.
