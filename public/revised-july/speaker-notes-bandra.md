# 02 — COMMERCIAL REVENUE PERFORMANCE

**[Transition smoothly.]**

Coming to revenue composition.

July produced **₹18.89 lakhs gross and ₹17.97 lakhs net across 231 transactions**, with an ATV of approximately **₹8,179**.

Memberships contributed approximately **45% of revenue**, and memberships plus class packages together accounted for approximately **89%**.

So the overwhelming majority of our revenue is still coming from the core fitness proposition.

That is healthy.

### Revenue quality

There is, however, an interesting shift in the mix.

The buyer base and transaction count were lower year-on-year, while average transaction value increased.

So July's growth came more from **value per customer** than simply having more customers.

**[Pause.]**

That's not necessarily a negative.

In fact, it shows that Bandra still has very good pricing power.

But over time, we want both:

**strong spend per customer and a growing customer base.**

So the focus is on retaining the higher spend levels while rebuilding buyer volume through stronger acquisition and retention.

### Memberships versus packages

We are also seeing more revenue moving toward larger class packages.

That is good from a cash-generation standpoint.

Where we can improve is in making sure those package buyers continue moving through the product ladder.

A package sale should not be viewed as the end of the journey.

It should be the beginning of the relationship.

**[Emphasise slightly.]**

Our commercial objective is therefore:

**sell the right entry product, get the client using it quickly, and then upgrade them into the right recurring relationship.**

### Major product contribution

One of the more notable findings in July is the contribution from large-value annual purchases.

A small number of high-value returning-client annual closes contributed materially to the month.

That tells us something important.

Our existing clients are willing to make significant commitments when the value proposition and timing are right.

So there is a very clear opportunity to build more deliberate upgrade and renewal conversations around our highest-intent customers.

### Seller performance

There is variation in both ATV and total production across the team.

Again, we are treating that as a coaching opportunity.

The objective isn't to create unnecessary competition within the desk.

It's to understand the behaviours behind the best results and make those behaviours repeatable.

**[Pause.]**

We also have a meaningful amount of online and self-service revenue, which is encouraging because it shows customers are comfortable completing purchases without always needing a manual close.

# 03 — ACQUISITION FUNNEL PERFORMANCE

**[More energetic delivery.]**

Acquisition is one of the most interesting areas in Bandra this month.

We generated **220 leads**, compared with **160 in June**.

That's a **37.5% increase month-on-month**.

We also had **153 first visits or trials**, up **50% versus June**.

So there is absolutely no shortage of activity entering the funnel.

### The real opportunity

The opportunity is improving the **quality and speed of conversion**.

Some sources are performing extremely well.

Others are creating a lot of apparent pipeline volume without translating into enough paying customers.

And that distinction is very important.

**[Pause.]**

For example, enquiries by phone showed very strong conversion.

Referrals, WhatsApp and other high-intent sources are also demonstrating better commercial behaviour.

That tells us where we should put more attention and budget.

### Hosted events

Hosted classes and pop-up activity generated significant lead volume.

That's positive in terms of awareness and brand exposure.

But the immediate commercial conversion from those events was comparatively low.

So we're refining rather than abandoning that channel.

**[Positive tone.]**

The plan is to use hosted events more selectively—particularly in off-peak periods—and redirect more acquisition energy toward channels that are generating stronger intent.

That means referrals, direct enquiries, WhatsApp and high-intent follow-up.

### Trial types

We had **63 standard trial classes**, **53 hosted classes**, **25 referral classes** and **11 paid first visits**.

Going forward, we're going to judge these trial types based not simply on how many people they bring in, but on what happens afterwards.

The relevant question is:

**Which trial experience produces the best member?**

### Instructor conversion

Instructor performance gives us another clear opportunity.

**Atulan**, for example, converted approximately **33% of his trial visits** and retained around **40%**.

**Reshma** and **Anmol** also show good retention outcomes from their trial cohorts, although on smaller samples.

Other instructors have more room to improve their newcomer results.

**[Pause.]**

The solution is straightforward.

We're going to place our strongest newcomer instructors in more key trial slots and use structured shadowing and coaching for the rest of the team.

That allows us to improve conversion without compromising anybody's long-term development.

### Follow-up process

There are also leads sitting in CRM stages such as **Trial Completed** without progressing quickly enough.

So we're tightening the process.

Every new lead should have:

**a call within 24 hours, WhatsApp by 48 hours, and a defined follow-up within three days.**

Every first visit should also have a short post-class desk consultation.

**[Emphasise.]**

The goal is to remove randomness from follow-up.

If somebody has shown enough interest to physically come into the studio, we should never lose them simply because nobody followed up properly.

It is a measured pilot.

We'll certify the instructors, launch into carefully selected slots, watch fill and trial behaviour, and expand only where the data supports it.

The aim is not simply to reduce anybody's schedule.

**[Positive emphasis.]**

# 05 — RETENTION & CHURN DYNAMICS

**[Slow down. More considered tone.]**

Retention is the area where the data deserves the most management attention.

July had **71 memberships reaching end-of-life**.

Of those, **43 renewed**, **19 lapsed** and **9 froze**.

That gives us a **60.6% renewal rate** and **26.8% churn**.

That is below where we want it to be.

But the most important part of the report is that it tells us **where the churn is being created before the customer actually leaves.**

### Active risk

We currently have **194 active clients**, of whom:

**78 are categorised as high risk,**

**24 as medium risk,**

and **92 as lower risk.**

That means slightly more than half of the active base shows some level of retention risk.

**[Pause.]**

That sounds like a large number, but it also gives us a highly actionable target list.

We don't have to guess whom to contact.

We know who they are.

### Attendance behaviour

The strongest behavioural signal is attendance frequency.

Historically, clients who lapse have averaged approximately **2.15 completed sessions**, compared with roughly **5.72 among renewers**.

We are also seeing risk cluster around specific class milestones, particularly around the seventh class.

That gives us a very practical intervention point.

**[Emphasise.]**

Instead of waiting for someone to stop coming entirely, we intervene when their usage pattern begins to weaken.

### High-risk outreach

For the **78 highest-risk active clients**, we're implementing structured outreach and a loyalty offer around the final days of package validity.

The target is to save approximately **10 to 12 accounts per month**.

Again, this isn't blanket discounting.

It's targeted retention spend where the likelihood and lifetime value justify intervention.

### Newcomer activation

We're also introducing a **Quick Starter** behaviour.

The goal is to encourage new customers to complete **three classes within their first 14 days**.

Because once someone establishes an early routine, their likelihood of staying increases materially.

So the reward is being tied to behaviour, not simply purchase.

### Lapsed-member pool

The cumulative lapsed pool now stands at approximately **347 members**.

Historically, that pool represents meaningful lifetime value.

So we are launching a rolling **90-day reactivation sequence**, with a target of bringing back **12 to 15 members per month**.

**[Pause.]**

This is one of the easier acquisition opportunities in the business because these people already know us.

The job is not to build awareness.

The job is to give them a relevant reason to return.

### Seller-linked retention

There is also significant variation in downstream retention by seller.

**Akshay Rane**, for example, has historically very strong retention among his clients.

**Jimmeey** is also strong.

That creates a useful internal benchmark.

Rather than looking only at who sells the most today, we're starting to ask:

**who creates customers who stay?**

That is a much more valuable sales-quality metric.

# 06 — DATA-DRIVEN RECOMMENDATIONS

**[More decisive tone.]**

The recommendations section turns all of this into a very practical operating plan.

There are a number of actions, but I would group them into five major priorities.

## 1. Rebuild the schedule around demand

First, we're going to reduce low-performing formats and free approximately **8 to 10 prime slots per week**.

At the same time, we're introducing a controlled Strength Lab pilot.

The objective is not to run fewer sessions for the sake of cost reduction.

The objective is to put the same operating effort into sessions that customers actually want.

**[Pause.]**

That should improve fill, instructor productivity and revenue per hour.

## 2. Improve the post-trial close

Second, every first visit will have a short desk consultation.

Follow-up will then happen at **24 and 48 hours**, supported by automated SMS or WhatsApp communication where appropriate.

We also have **41 Trial Completed leads** currently sitting in the pipeline.

Those will be worked immediately.

The target is simple:

**zero qualified trial lead sitting untouched for more than seven days.**

## 3. Reallocate acquisition toward stronger channels

Third, we're becoming more selective with hosted events.

They are useful for awareness, but we need to be disciplined about how much of the funnel they represent if their conversion remains low.

So hosted pop-ups will be limited, and more energy will go into referrals, WhatsApp and direct enquiries.

We want the funnel to be large.

But we also want it to be **high intent.**

## 4. Create a proactive retention engine

Fourth, we're activating the high-risk membership list before expiration.

That includes targeted loyalty offers, newcomer graduation journeys and the three-classes-in-14-days activation rule.

We're also beginning the 90-day lapsed-member reactivation campaign.

So retention becomes an ongoing operating process rather than a month-end rescue exercise.

## 5. Strengthen data hygiene

Finally, there are some CRM and POS hygiene items that need to be cleaned up.

There are **134 CRM records showing Membership Sold with no matching purchase value**, and an Unknown Class mapping issue affecting several sessions.

These don't necessarily mean commercial performance is wrong.

But they reduce confidence in attribution.

So they're being reconciled against POS and corrected with the relevant systems teams.

**[Pause.]**

I would rather identify those inconsistencies and fix them now than allow inaccurate data to become embedded in future decision-making.
