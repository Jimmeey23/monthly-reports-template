# 02 — COMMERCIAL REVENUE PERFORMANCE

**[Move to next section.]**

Coming to the commercial performance in a little more detail.

July generated **₹24.54 lakhs of net revenue across 349 transactions**, with an average transaction value of approximately **₹7,393**.

The important thing here is the composition.

**Memberships contributed roughly 47% of revenue**, and memberships together with class packages accounted for approximately **81% of total revenue**.

That gives us a reasonably diversified revenue base.

**[Pause.]**

One thing we are watching is average transaction value.

Transaction volume increased strongly, while ATV softened.

I actually see that as quite an understandable outcome of the mix we generated in July.

We had more single classes and newcomer offers entering the business, so naturally the initial ticket size is lower.

The job now is to **convert those lower-ticket entry customers into higher-value packages and memberships.**

So rather than looking at the ATV decline in isolation, we're looking at it as a **monetisation opportunity created by higher customer volume.**

### Sales by category

On category mix, memberships continue to be the core revenue engine.

That is positive because it gives us better revenue visibility and a stronger relationship with the client.

At the same time, class packages are contributing meaningfully, which gives customers flexibility without making us dependent on one product.

Our focus now is on moving clients through that ladder:

**trial → introductory product → package → membership.**

That progression is going to be increasingly important.

### Top products

At product level, we continue to see concentration in some of the larger packages and memberships.

That is useful because it tells us where the customer is willing to make a meaningful commitment.

**[Pause.]**

The opportunity is to make that upgrade conversation more systematic rather than leaving it entirely to individual selling styles.

So we're working toward clearer upgrade prompts and better follow-up around package milestones.

### Seller performance

On seller attribution, there is naturally some variation across the team.

I don't think the right response is simply to label somebody as a strong or weak seller.

What the data gives us is an opportunity to understand **what the best performers are doing differently** and make that repeatable across the team.

So we're introducing more structured coaching, peer learning and closer coverage during our highest-value sales windows.

The objective is simple:

**raise the floor of the team while continuing to support the people who are already performing strongly.**

### Payment mix

Our payment mix is also reasonably diversified.

The majority is currently coming through the custom payment category, followed by cash and Stripe.

We will continue to monitor this because payment method has a direct impact on processing cost and reconciliation.

Where we can move transactions toward efficient, low-fee methods without affecting the customer experience, we will.

### When revenue happens

**[Slow slightly—important operational point.]**

One of the most useful findings in this section is **when we actually make money.**

Three clock-hours—**8 to 9 AM, 11 AM to 12 PM, and 12 to 1 PM—generated approximately ₹12.22 lakhs**, or about **47% of the month's revenue**.

That is extremely actionable.

It tells us that our staffing cannot be uniform throughout the day.

Our strongest closers need to be available when the commercial opportunity is highest.

Saturday is another important commitment day.

So we're aligning front-desk and sales coverage much more closely to those revenue windows.

**[Pause.]**

There are also two data and compliance items that require verification: the GST treatment reflected in the data and the duplicated transaction-ID issue in the POS records.

I don't want to speculate on the GST position until it has been confirmed by the appropriate finance and tax advisers.

What matters operationally is that **both items have been identified, and we're getting them validated and corrected rather than allowing uncertainty to carry forward.**

# 03 — ACQUISITION FUNNEL PERFORMANCE

**[Slightly more energetic.]**

Moving to acquisition.

July generated **154 leads**, which produced **121 trials, 35 conversions and 35 retained clients**.

The resulting trial conversion rate was **28.9%**.

And there is a very important distinction here.

**Our primary constraint is not that people are trying us and rejecting the product.**

Our bigger opportunity is generating **more qualified leads at the top of the funnel.**

Leads were actually up **20.3% month-on-month**, which is encouraging.

Conversions were lower month-on-month, but the underlying conversion rate remains healthy relative to our broader 2026 performance.

So the response isn't to reinvent the trial experience.

The response is to **feed more qualified prospects into something that is already converting reasonably well—and tighten the follow-up around it.**

### Lead sources

Referral is particularly interesting.

It is our **highest-quality lead source**, with conversion around **45.5%**.

That is a very strong signal.

**[Pause.]**

Referral leads arrive with trust already built into the relationship.

So we're putting more structure behind referrals rather than treating them as something that happens organically.

The recommendation is to increase referral contribution materially and use a clear referral credit to encourage advocacy.

### Trial mix

On trial types, we're also looking at which entry products create the best downstream behaviour.

Not every trial should be judged purely on whether somebody attended.

The better question is:

**Did that trial create a member?**

So we are moving toward measuring trial products based on conversion and retention quality rather than volume alone.

### Instructor-led conversion

Instructor performance also matters here.

There is variation in the conversion and retention outcomes associated with different instructors.

Rather than treating that negatively, we see this as a very practical coaching opportunity.

We know there are instructors whose newcomer experience is producing stronger downstream behaviour.

So we're going to use those instructors more deliberately in key trial slots, while having other instructors shadow them and adopt the same successful behaviours.

**[Pause.]**

The immediate operational change is also very simple:

**every trial gets a structured follow-up within 48 hours.**

No ambiguity, no dependence on memory, and no trial quietly disappearing from the pipeline.

# 04 — DELIVERY, FORMATS & INSTRUCTOR PERFORMANCE

**[Transition.]**

Moving to studio delivery.

July had **368 sessions and 2,432 visits**, giving us an overall fill rate of **47.3%** and an average of approximately **6.6 visits per session**.

The first thing I would point out is that our schedule has become considerably more diversified.

We are no longer operating essentially as a single-format studio.

We now have a meaningful mix across **Barre, Cycle and Strength**.

And the encouraging part is that the diversification is working.

### Format performance

Strength is particularly strong.

**Studio Strength Lab Full Body reached an 89% fill rate**, and PowerCycle Express is also operating at very high utilisation.

Those are not demand problems.

Those are effectively **capacity opportunities.**

Where we see sustained utilisation at those levels, we should be asking whether we can intelligently add supply.

**[Pause.]**

At the other end, we have some formats and slots with lower utilisation.

Again, the solution isn't to react emotionally and remove classes immediately.

We're using a **three-week minimum-class-size rule**.

If a recurring slot consistently fails to attract sufficient demand, we can consolidate it and reallocate that instructor time into a format or time slot where customers are asking for more capacity.

So the schedule becomes progressively smarter.

### Class-level performance

This is really the principle we're applying:

**protect what customers clearly want, test what is new, and prune what repeatedly doesn't earn its place on the schedule.**

There is also an “Unknown Class” classification appearing in the data.

At **2.2% fill**, that looks alarming if read literally, but the more important issue is that the classification itself suggests a data-mapping problem.

So we're fixing the underlying mapping rather than drawing operational conclusions from a category we don't fully trust.

### Instructor performance

Instructor performance gives us another useful layer.

Some instructors are producing strong attendance and retention outcomes, while others have more opportunity.

Our response is coaching and deployment.

We're pairing instructors with strong newcomer and retention outcomes with colleagues who can benefit from observing their approach.

We're also adjusting session allocation where the data shows that the current load isn't producing the best result.

**[Positive tone.]**

That isn't about taking opportunity away from people.

It's about putting instructors into environments where they can perform at their best and giving them the support to improve.

### Heatmap and scheduling

The day-and-time heatmap gives us the final piece.

Demand is not evenly distributed.

So the schedule shouldn't be evenly distributed either.

We're using actual attendance patterns to gradually shift capacity toward stronger windows and eliminate recurring sessions with very low attendance.

If we do this well, we can improve overall fill without needing significantly more customer demand.

# 05 — RETENTION & CHURN DYNAMICS

**[Slow down slightly.]**

Retention is probably one of the most important sections of this report.

In July, **120 memberships reached end-of-life**.

**83 renewed, 27 lapsed and 10 were frozen.**

That gives us a **69.2% renewal rate** and **22.5% churn**.

The encouraging part is that churn improved substantially versus June.

So the direction is positive.

Now we need to make that improvement systematic.

### Where lapses are concentrated

The lapse data shows us that the issue isn't evenly distributed across every product.

There is meaningful concentration in specific packages—particularly the **Studio 4 Class Package**.

That is useful because a concentrated problem is much easier to address than a completely random one.

We can target those customers with specific renewal and upgrade journeys.

### The lapsed-member opportunity

Our cumulative lapsed database now contains **482 unique members**.

I don't look at that simply as historical churn.

I look at it as an **existing audience that already knows the brand.**

They don't need to be educated about who we are from zero.

Some of them need the right reason and the right timing to come back.

So we're building a more structured reactivation programme rather than leaving those names sitting passively in the CRM.

### Active risk pool

Even more importantly, we have an active risk pool of **101 clients**.

This is where we can become much more proactive.

Instead of waiting for somebody to lapse and then trying to win them back, we can identify declining attendance **before renewal becomes a problem.**

For example, when a member's attendance drops below a defined threshold, that should trigger an intervention:

a coach check-in, a front-desk conversation, a class recommendation, or simply a personal message.

**[Pause.]**

Retention should start **before the renewal date**, not on the renewal date.

That is the operating behaviour we're moving toward.

### Seller-linked retention

We're also looking at retention by seller.

Again, this isn't intended as a blame metric.

It's a quality metric.

A good sale isn't simply one that closes today.

A good sale creates a customer who uses the product, sees value and stays.

So we're beginning to connect front-end sales performance with downstream customer retention.

That should ultimately improve both the customer experience and the economics of the studio.

# 06 — DATA-DRIVEN RECOMMENDATIONS

**[More decisive tone.]**

This section is where the report becomes an execution plan.

There are several individual actions, but they really come down to a small number of management priorities.

### 1. Optimise the class schedule

First, we're going to continue moving capacity toward high-demand formats and reducing recurring low-utilisation slots.

Strength and selected Cycle formats have clearly earned additional attention.

Lower-performing slots will be monitored over a defined period before changes are made.

The objective is not simply fewer classes.

The objective is **a more productive schedule.**

### 2. Maintain pricing and discount discipline

Second, we're keeping tight control over discounting.

July's discount penetration was approximately **6.6%**, which is comfortably below the report's 10% red-flag threshold.

So we're not trying to eliminate discounting.

We're making sure every discount has a commercial reason.

Larger discounts will require approval, and we're reviewing discount behaviour at SKU level.

Where possible, I would much rather give customers **additional value** than simply reduce price.

### 3. Grow and tighten the funnel

Third, we need more leads.

The target is to take the lead pipeline toward at least **184 leads per month**, while maintaining conversion quality.

Referral is a major part of that.

And every trial will be followed up within **48 hours**.

That gives us both sides of the equation:

**more opportunities entering the funnel and fewer opportunities leaking out of it.**

### 4. Build a retention engine

Fourth, we're moving retention from reactive to proactive.

That means:

early-warning attendance triggers,

structured renewal conversations,

targeted reactivation of lapsed members,

and better tracking of retention by product, instructor and seller.

The objective isn't simply to win back churn.

It's to prevent avoidable churn before it happens.

### 5. Improve operational productivity

Fifth, we're tightening operating discipline around cancellations, check-ins and utilisation.

We recorded **295 late cancellations** in July.

That is capacity which looked occupied but wasn't ultimately used.

We're going to monitor that behaviour carefully and apply clearer policies where necessary.

The goal is not to penalise customers unnecessarily.

The goal is to make scarce class capacity available to customers who actually want to attend.

### Instructor and sales deployment

We're also making deployment more data-led.

High-performing instructors will play a larger role in newcomer and high-conversion slots.

Other instructors will get structured shadowing and coaching.

Similarly, our strongest commercial coverage will sit around the hours where revenue is actually being captured—particularly **8 AM, 11 AM, 12 PM and Saturday mornings.**

### Retail

Retail is another relatively straightforward opportunity.

We have **2,432 monthly studio visits**.

Every one of those is an opportunity for relevant product discovery.

We don't need aggressive selling.

We need better placement, better visibility and a natural front-desk script.

Even modest improvement in revenue per visit can become meaningful at that level of footfall.

### Compliance and data

Finally, we're cleaning up the reporting infrastructure itself.

The GST treatment requires professional verification, and the POS transaction-ID issue needs to be corrected with the vendor.

The important point is that these issues are visible.

We're not building management decisions on assumptions.

Where something requires validation, we're validating it.
